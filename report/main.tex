Summarize the warehouse problem, the approaches evaluated, and the main

\section*{Using this template}
Each section describes the content it should cover. You are free to organize
the discussion within it. Replace the guidance with your analysis and update
the title, authors, and affiliation. The generated evidence examples at the
end demonstrate how code outputs can be included in the report.

For the abstract, IEEE recommends a self-contained paragraph of up to
250 words~\cite{ieee-structure}. The UW--Madison guide explains how to connect
the purpose, methods, findings, and implications~\cite{uw-abstract}.

\section{Introduction}
Introduce the warehouse and the operational questions being investigated.
State the scope and the criteria used to assess proposed approaches, such as
travel, space, throughput, service, and implementation effort. Explain how
activity profiling informs the storage, layout, and order picking analyses,
and how the final comparison weighs their benefits, limitations, and
interactions. Establish the purpose of the evaluation while leaving its
outcome to the analysis.

\section{Data and evaluation methods}
Describe the data, observation periods, units, and checks that support the
analysis. Explain the common baseline, performance measures, assumptions,
and experimental settings needed to interpret and reproduce the comparisons.
Present details specific to an approach where that approach is evaluated.
Cite the sources supporting the methods and make the reported results
traceable to the saved evidence~\cite{turingway}. Keep installation and
execution instructions in the README.

\section{Warehouse activity profiling}
Characterize the current operation using warehouse activity, customer-order,
item-activity, and inventory profiles, together with relevant performance
indicators. Explain the patterns, imbalances, or constraints revealed by the
evidence and their implications for subsequent design choices. Define the
scope, units, and calculation of each measure. Select tables and figures
that support the diagnosis and explain what they show.

\section{Storage assignment and space requirements}
Explain the proposed storage approaches and how they determine product
locations and space requirements. Describe the model, parameters, assumptions,
and constraints needed to evaluate them. Compare their performance with the
current assignment on a consistent basis, supported by quantitative results
and a readable layout. Discuss capacity, compatibility, replenishment, and
service, and identify the conditions under which each approach is suitable.

\section{Warehouse layout design}
Explain the layout alternatives and the space, storage, throughput, and
handling requirements they address. Show the arrangements and flows clearly,
with the scale and assumptions needed to interpret them. Evaluate relevant
performance measures against the baseline and discuss operational feasibility,
including access, safety, replenishment, staging, and equipment. Explain the
benefits, limitations, and implementation implications of the alternatives.

\section{Order picking system design}
Describe the proposed picking approaches and why they suit the observed
workload. Explain the relevant process rules, resources, and interactions
with storage and layout. Evaluate the alternatives under comparable operating
conditions using appropriate performance measures. Discuss uncertainty,
service, replenishment, and implementation effort, and explain the conditions
under which the approaches are feasible and useful.

\section{Performance evaluation and trade-offs}
Bring together the storage, layout, and picking findings using consistent
baselines, demand, units, and constraints. Weigh their effects on performance,
space, service, workload, cost, and risk where the evidence permits. Examine
how the approaches interact when combined, including benefits or burdens
shifted between processes. Explain how uncertainty and data limitations affect
the comparison, then justify a suitable combination or sequence of changes
and the conditions for adopting it.

\section{Conclusion}
Answer the overall evaluation question using the strongest findings. State
the recommended course of action, the main quantitative evidence supporting
it, and the conditions or limitations that matter to the decision. Identify
any further evidence or monitoring needed before implementation. Keep the
conclusion consistent with the preceding analysis.
